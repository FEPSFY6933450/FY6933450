Introduction

        Advancements in both electronics and neuroscience have allowed for the possibility of brain computer interfaces being used to aid in the use of prosthetics for patients with spinal injuries and other use cases in the foreseeable future. 
        Brain computer interfaces or "brain chips" translate electrical signals that are produced on " the scalp, on the cortical surface or within the brain without the participation of peripheral nerves and muscles 
        (McFarland & Wolpaw, 2011, Introduction).The electrical signal are then translated to inputs that a peripheral can interpret. This allows for user's intentions to be undrtst and preformed near instantaneously rather than a user having to physically interact with a component or contract a muscle.
